**Publication:** Harvard Business School Institute for Business in Global Society  
**Date:** June 26, 2024

🏛️ **Feature Article:** Our pioneering research on electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure, co-authored with my advisor Dr. Omar Asensio, was featured in an article by the Harvard Business School's Institute for Business in Global Society (BiGS) titled "**The state of EV charging in America: Harvard research shows chargers 78% reliable and pricing like the ‘Wild West’**."

🔍 **Key Insights:**

Our study represents a **first-ever examination** of over **1 million EV charging station reviews** by drivers across North America, Europe, and Asia over a decade. Utilizing customized artificial intelligence models, we extracted critical insights into the challenges faced by EV drivers, uncovering significant obstacles to EV adoption and carbon emission reduction in the United States.

**Main Findings:**

- **Reliability Issues:** Public EV charging stations in the U.S. have an average reliability score of only **78%**, meaning approximately one in five charging attempts fails due to broken or malfunctioning equipment. This reliability is notably lower than that of traditional gas stations.

- **Pricing Confusion:** EV drivers experience erratic and opaque pricing models, often likened to the "Wild West." Without standardized or transparent pricing information, drivers face unexpected costs and cannot easily compare charging options.

- **Charging Deserts:** There is an uneven distribution of public charging stations, leading to "charging deserts" in certain rural and small urban areas. This lack of infrastructure poses equity concerns and hinders EV adoption in underserved communities.

- **Driver Frustrations:** Issues such as internal combustion engine vehicles occupying charging spots ("getting ICE'd") and insufficient maintenance of charging stations contribute to driver dissatisfaction.

💡 **Impact and Opportunities:**

Our research highlights critical areas where improvements are necessary to support the widespread adoption of EVs:

- **Business Opportunities:** The identified challenges present opportunities for entrepreneurs and businesses to innovate solutions for charging infrastructure reliability, maintenance services, and transparent pricing models.

- **Policy Implications:** Insights from our study can inform policymakers to develop regulations and incentives that promote the expansion and standardization of EV charging networks, ensuring equitable access and supporting national goals for reducing carbon emissions.

- **Consumer Empowerment:** By voicing the experiences of current EV drivers, our research empowers consumers and brings attention to real-world issues that need addressing to enhance the overall EV ownership experience.
